1. PROJECT OVERVIEW

1.1 Product Vision

Arbit is a comprehensive, data-driven Fantasy Football companion application designed specifically for the Family Football Association (FFA), a private family ESPN Fantasy Football redraft league.

The goal is to create a premium-quality, deeply analytical web application that functions as an all-in-one fantasy football management, research, and decision-support platform.

Arbit should act as an intelligent advisor across virtually every responsibility, activity, and strategic decision involved in fantasy football.

Rather than serving as a basic fantasy football dashboard or simply displaying information already available through ESPN, Arbit should transform extensive football data into actionable, contextually relevant insights.

The application should provide advanced analysis, recommendations, projections, interactive visualizations, and personalized management tools that help FFA members make informed decisions throughout the season.

This includes the draft, weekly lineup management, waiver wire acquisitions, trade negotiations, player research, live NFL games, league matchups, and historical league performance.

Arbit’s central purpose is to turn comprehensive football data into useful fantasy football intelligence.

1.2 Core Product Principles

Development should be guided by the following principles:

1. Data-driven intelligence: Statistical evidence and analytical models should form the foundation of Arbit’s recommendations.
2. League-specific context: Recommendations must account for FFA’s actual league configuration, scoring settings, roster requirements, teams, and player availability.
3. Comprehensive analysis: Arbit should utilize the full breadth of relevant football statistics rather than relying solely on basic statistics, conventional fantasy rankings, or third-party fantasy projections.
4. Actionable recommendations: The application should explain what actions are worth considering and provide supporting evidence for its conclusions.
5. Unified intelligence: Features should share underlying data, analytical systems, player evaluations, and league context rather than functioning as disconnected tools.
6. High-quality user experience: Arbit should feel like a polished, cohesive, purpose-built product with a comprehensive design system.
7. Maintainable architecture: Development must emphasize consistency, documentation, modularity, and long-term maintainability.
8. Agentic development continuity: Any authorized AI coding agent should be able to understand the project’s current architecture, implementation history, decisions, and outstanding work through standardized project documentation.

2. PRODUCT IDENTITY & NAMING

2.1 Official Identity

Product Name: Arbit

Descriptive Subtitle: FFA Fantasy Football Companion

Primary Management Workspace: Front Office

The name Arbit is derived from the linguistic family of arbiter, reflecting judgment, evaluation, and decision-making.

The product should retain Arbit as its independent identity rather than making FFA part of its official name.

FFA identifies the league that Arbit serves.

2.2 Product Feature Family

Arbit will be organized around a cohesive family of major capabilities.

Feature	Primary Responsibility
Arbit Front Office	Personalized manager headquarters and central management workspace
Arbit Draft	Draft preparation, simulations, and live draft assistance
Arbit Scout	Player research, statistics, profiles, comparisons, and archetypes
Arbit Market	Waiver wire analysis, free agents, and trade intelligence
Arbit Lineup	Sit/start recommendations, roster optimization, and matchup analysis
Arbit Live	NFL game tracking, live scores, play-by-play, and interactive game visualizations

3. LEAGUE CONTEXT & APPLICATION SCOPE

3.1 FFA League

Arbit is being developed for one existing ESPN Fantasy Football league.

League Name: Family Football Association
League Abbreviation: FFA
League Platform: ESPN Fantasy Football
League Format: Redraft
League Status: Currently in its inaugural season

The application should retrieve and maintain a complete understanding of the FFA league.

This includes the league’s configuration, teams, managers, rosters, matchups, and all other relevant information available through ESPN.

3.2 Single-League Scope

The initial application will support one fantasy football league: FFA.

The possibility of attaching additional leagues may be considered after development is completed.

However, future multi-league functionality is not part of the current development scope and should not influence the initial product requirements or architectural priorities.

Arbit should be designed and developed to provide the best possible experience for its intended FFA users.

3.3 User Experience

FFA members should be able to log into Arbit and access a personalized experience relevant to their respective fantasy football teams.

The application should recognize each member’s team, roster, matchup, and league context.

This personalization should extend throughout the application, including draft recommendations, player research, roster assessments, lineup decisions, waiver wire analysis, and trade suggestions.

3.4 Championship Tradition

FFA’s championship trophy is a replica of the Geller Cup from the television series Friends.

Each season:

* The winning team’s name and championship year are added to the trophy.
* The winning manager holds the trophy throughout the offseason.
* The league conducts a new redraft for the following season.

Arbit Archive should provide a natural home for the league’s developing history, including previous champions, season records, and Geller Cup history.

Because this is the league’s first season, historical league information will accumulate over time.

4.2 Arbit Draft — End-to-End Draft Intelligence

Arbit Draft will provide a comprehensive, contextually aware Live Draft Assistant.

The goal is to support the user throughout the entire draft process, from preparation through the final selection.

Core Requirements

The assistant must understand:

* FFA’s roster configuration and position requirements.
* All applicable league scoring rules.
* League size and draft structure.
* The user’s assigned draft position.
* The user’s current draft selections.
* Positions already filled.
* Remaining roster requirements.
* Currently available players.
* Players selected by other teams.
* Positional scarcity and draft priorities.
* Relevant player performance statistics.
* Player outlooks and projected fantasy value.
* Bye-week distributions and potential roster conflicts.
* The probability of individual players being drafted in particular rounds or at particular picks.

Draft Recommendations

The assistant should continuously evaluate the current draft state and provide data-backed recommendations.

Its recommendations should account for the user’s existing roster composition, remaining needs, league scoring system, player availability, and expected draft behavior.

Draft suggestions should not simply reproduce a static ranking or best-player-available list.

They should be situationally aware, updating as the draft progresses.

Draft Intelligence Capabilities

Arbit Draft should support:

* Pre-draft preparation and player evaluation.
* Draft strategy analysis.
* Draft simulations and scenario evaluation.
* Live draft tracking.
* Dynamic player recommendations.
* Positional priority analysis.
* Roster construction evaluation.
* Bye-week considerations.
* Expected player availability by round and pick.
* Comparisons between available draft options.
* Data-supported explanations of recommended selections.

The ultimate goal is an end-to-end experience that allows the user to make informed selections at every stage of an FFA draft.

The advisor must examine:

* The user’s complete fantasy roster.
* The current FFA matchup between the two competing fantasy teams.
* Relevant NFL matchups for each player.
* Individual player performance statistics.
* Recent performance trends.
* NFL team and opponent statistics.
* Position-versus-defense performance.
* Opposing defensive strengths and weaknesses.

* Applicable league scoring settings.
* Other relevant football statistics and contextual information.

Sit/Start Recommendations

Arbit Lineup should provide direct recommendations on which players to start and which to bench.

The recommendations should be generated from comprehensive statistical analysis rather than simply relying on fantasy platform projections.

Users should be able to understand why one player is favored over another.

Intended Capabilities

* Weekly starting lineup recommendations.
* Individual start/sit comparisons.
* Matchup-based player analysis.
* Statistical support for each recommendation.
* Comparative player outlooks.
* Roster and positional evaluation.
* Model-generated projections and confidence assessments.
* Contextual consideration of the user’s fantasy opponent.

The primary objective is to provide deeply researched and understandable lineup advice.

4.4 Arbit Market — Waiver Wire & Trade Intelligence

Arbit Market will serve as the primary workspace for roster improvements through player acquisitions, waiver wire activity, and trades.

It will contain two major advisory capabilities.

A. Waiver Wire Advisor

The Waiver Wire Advisor will continuously evaluate the players available for acquisition in FFA.

Its analysis should consider both current availability and evolving player value.

Core Requirements

* Retrieve currently available free agents and waiver-eligible players.
* Monitor player availability throughout the season.
* Evaluate recent and historical player performance.
* Identify meaningful statistical trends.
* Assess upcoming NFL matchups.
* Evaluate player opportunity and projected performance.
* Compare available players against the user’s existing roster.
* Identify positional weaknesses and improvement opportunities.
* Account for FFA’s scoring and roster rules.

Intended Capabilities

* Weekly waiver recommendations.
* Updated recommendations as relevant data changes.
* Position-specific waiver evaluations.
* Player comparisons.
* Potential roster upgrade identification.
* Statistical reasoning for recommended pickups.

The Waiver Wire Advisor should focus on identifying acquisitions that provide meaningful value to the user’s fantasy team.

B. Trade Assistant

The Trade Assistant will evaluate player values and team needs throughout the FFA league to identify advantageous and realistic trade opportunities.

Core Requirements

The Trade Assistant must:

* Analyze the complete rosters of every FFA team.
* Continuously evaluate relevant player statistics.
* Understand positional strengths and weaknesses across teams.
* Identify the user’s roster deficiencies.
* Identify opposing managers’ potential roster needs.
* Account for bye-week gaps and positional depth.
* Assess the value of players involved in potential trades.
* Compare the benefit of a trade for both managers.
* Identify trade proposals that offer meaningful roster improvements.
* Estimate trade feasibility and likelihood of acceptance.

Intended Capabilities

* Trade opportunity discovery.
* Suggested trade packages.
* Player value comparisons.
* Team-to-team roster compatibility analysis.
* Identification of mutually beneficial trades.
* Statistical evaluation of proposed transactions.
* Recommendations for the most promising trade opportunities.

The objective is not merely to label trades as fair or unfair.

Arbit should actively identify valuable opportunities based on the specific rosters, needs, and circumstances of FFA managers.

Arbit Live will provide an interactive NFL game tracking experience combining live scores, play-by-play information, fantasy relevance, and graphical representations of on-field action.

* Current game status.
* Team information.

* Player participation and performance.
* Fantasy-relevant scoring events.

Arbit Live should incorporate interactive football field graphics capable of visually representing individual plays.

* Passing plays.
* Rushing plays.
* Quarterback sneaks.
* Sacks.
* Interceptions.
* Field goals.
* Point-after-touchdown attempts.
* Other applicable football play types.

* Football field representations.
* NFL team uniform displays.
* Matchup-specific visuals.

Examples include:

* Displaying only plays involving the user’s rostered players.
* Identifying fantasy-relevant events.
* Following the performance of individual players.
* Connecting NFL game activity to the user’s fantasy matchup.

Arbit Live should offer a meaningful combination of NFL game information and fantasy football context.

4.7 Arbit Model — Shared Football Intelligence

Arbit Model is the shared analytical foundation supporting Arbit’s decision-making features.

Rather than constructing an entirely separate analytical system for each advisor, Arbit should employ reusable data models, statistical methodologies, projections, and player evaluations.

Responsibilities

Arbit Model should support:

* Comprehensive statistical evaluation.
* Historical player analysis.
* Player performance projections.
* Player rankings.
* Advanced player comparisons.
* Player archetype classification.
* Positional and matchup analysis.
* Statistical trends.
* Roster value assessments.
* Draft evaluations.
* Sit/start recommendations.
* Waiver evaluations.
* Trade intelligence.
* Machine learning where appropriate.

Modeling Philosophy

The primary data foundation for Arbit Model must be the nflverse suite of datasets.

Analytical systems should utilize meaningful NFL data and advanced statistical relationships rather than relying exclusively on conventional fantasy projections or basic box-score statistics.

Supplementary sources may be used to address genuine gaps in available information.

Model development should take into account the purpose of each recommendation and the relevant FFA league context.

Shared Intelligence Architecture

The same underlying football data and player evaluations should be reusable across Arbit’s major modules.

For example, Arbit Draft, Arbit Scout, Arbit Market, and Arbit Lineup should not maintain conflicting or unrelated implementations of core player statistical analysis.

Each feature may apply its own decision-specific logic while drawing upon shared foundational intelligence.

5. DATA SOURCES & INTEGRATION REQUIREMENTS

The success of Arbit depends heavily on the quality, completeness, consistency, and availability of its underlying data.

Development should establish a reliable data ingestion, normalization, storage, and updating architecture.

The following source priorities are fundamental to the project.

5.1 ESPN Fantasy Football — Primary League Authority

ESPN is the authoritative source for all FFA-specific fantasy league information.

Arbit must continuously retrieve, update, and maintain the available league information necessary to power its features.

Required League Data

This includes, but is not limited to:

* League identification and metadata.
* League configuration.
* League size.
* Roster formats.
* Roster position requirements.
* Starting lineup rules.
* All applicable scoring settings.
* Participating teams.
* Team managers.
* Complete team rosters.
* Player ownership.
* Available free agents.
* Waiver information.
* League matchups.
* League standings.
* Draft information.
* Other available league-specific information.

The integration should use the relevant ESPN Fantasy Football data interfaces, including unofficial ESPN API endpoints where needed.

The objective is to maintain a comprehensive, up-to-date understanding of the FFA league throughout Arbit.

5.2 nflverse — Primary NFL Statistical Foundation

The nflverse suite of databases will be Arbit’s primary source for NFL statistical data.

This is a core development requirement.

The nflverse ecosystem should provide the primary statistical foundation for:

* Player performance analysis.
* Team performance analysis.
* Advanced football metrics.
* Historical research.
* Player evaluations.
* Statistical projections.
* Player archetypes.
* Positional analysis.
* Machine learning and model training.
* Data-driven fantasy recommendations.

Source Priority

Arbit’s analytical systems should be developed around nflverse data first.

Other datasets and information sources may supplement nflverse where relevant information is missing or where the particular application requires additional data.

Third-party fantasy projections should not replace the deeper statistical evaluation and modeling intended for Arbit.

5.3 Sleeper API — Supplementary Scoring & Play Data

Arbit will utilize the Sleeper API as a supplementary football data source.

Sleeper will not be implemented as an additional fantasy league provider during the initial development.

The intended purpose of its API integration is to retrieve applicable scoring, player, and game-related information that can support Arbit’s features.

The project owner has previously worked with the Sleeper API for related data retrieval.

The developer should research the current available endpoints and incorporate useful information into the appropriate data pipelines.

5.4 Unofficial ESPN API — Live Data Research Priority

For gaps in live NFL game information, scores, and play-by-play data, the development process should first research relevant unofficial ESPN API resources.

This research should determine which live event details and associated game information are available.

If additional sources are needed for particular live game capabilities, they may be evaluated afterward.

The objective is to assemble the necessary information for Arbit Live’s scoring, play-by-play, fantasy tracking, and graphical event representations.

5.5 Gridiron Databases — Field & Uniform Assets

Gridiron databases are the intended source for football field and NFL uniform visual assets.

There is currently no official API identified for the intended integration.

The project owner has previously retrieved field and uniform resources using discovered direct-download URL structures.

Development should account for the available asset retrieval methods and their integration into Arbit Live.

The intended result is a visual environment that can display appropriate field representations and NFL team uniforms for the relevant matchups and weeks.

6. Handoff: Provide a clear summary of completed work, remaining tasks, and information necessary for the next development session or agent.

Where possible, the repository and development tooling should help enforce these standardized procedures.

* Feature boundaries.
* Major user workflows.
* Data requirements.
* System interactions.
* Module dependencies.
* Proposed implementation milestones.
* Validation and acceptance criteria.

Ensure the resulting specification retains the requirements described in this document.

Step 4 — UX/UI & Design System Plan

Establish the initial design system proposal and product information architecture.

Step 5 — Agentic Development Framework

Design and establish the standardized development workflow, documentation structure, coding guidelines, implementation gates, and agent handoff procedures.

This framework must be suitable for use by multiple AI coding agents across different LLM providers.

Step 6 — Project Scaffold & Development Roadmap

Prepare a project scaffold aligned with the approved architecture and governance standards.

Create a detailed implementation roadmap that identifies the foundational work and the dependencies required to begin development.

12. DEFINITION OF SUCCESS

Arbit will ultimately be successful when it provides FFA league members with a unified, powerful, and intuitive environment for managing their fantasy football experience.

A family member should be able to log into Arbit and access comprehensive information about their team, players, matchups, and league.

They should be able to research players in substantial detail, receive well-supported strategic recommendations, evaluate roster improvements, prepare for drafts, follow live NFL action, and understand the reasoning behind the application’s advice.

The platform should combine:

* The league-specific context of ESPN Fantasy Football.
* The statistical depth of nflverse.
* Additional relevant football information.
* Purpose-built analytical and machine-learning systems.
* Rich interactive user experiences.
* A consistent product design.
* Maintainable, well-documented engineering.

From a development standpoint, success also means that the codebase, architecture, and documentation remain coherent as the project grows, including when implementation transitions between different Agentic AI developers.

The ultimate objective is to build Arbit into a comprehensive fantasy football intelligence and management platform that the FFA family can rely on throughout every phase of the fantasy football season.
