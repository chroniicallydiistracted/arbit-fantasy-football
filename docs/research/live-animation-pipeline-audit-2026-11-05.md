# Live animation pipeline audit · November 5, 2026

## Scope and evidence

M6 requirements LV-001 through LV-005. The audit followed NFL detail normalization and stored revisions through play facts, choreography, pacing, scoreboard generation, API serialization, private art, canvas drawing and browser playback. ESPN was included where its postgame read supplied the current stored replay.

- Weeks 1 and 8 supplied 5,709 archived NFL v2 records in 30 games. For Week 9, the database supplied the current Jacksonville–Baltimore replay and the latest NFL revision per play as of its confirmed final observation. The 166 historical NFL rows and their four attached tries reproduce the counts from the [earlier raw coverage audit](live-play-coverage-2026-11-05.md). The historical selection is per play ID, so it does not establish a byte-identical reconstruction of the full HTTP response.
- The production animation builder and response models processed each game. All 4,919 NFL scripts, including 326 filtered from playback, went through the applicable existing motion, alignment, realism and outcome rules. The same checks ran separately on the 144 scripts from the current mixed-source Thursday replay.
- Chromium drew 2,912 sampled frames across all 420 clip instances in three replay sets. The three sets were Chicago–Seattle, the current Jacksonville–Baltimore replay and its NFL history replay. They represent two games with matching private art. Frames included the start, snap, ball flights, whistle, result cards and end. The isolated web app also replayed controlled new-play and correction events through the regular event listeners.
- This was a completed-game replay audit. It measures script semantics and browser behavior under the recorded and controlled cases. Live arrival performance, every frame of every clip, and cross-browser rendering remain unmeasured.

## Cross-checks and limits

- All 30 archived final scores agreed with the final board state. The Thursday current and NFL-history replays both agreed with the NFL summary, 33–10. Eight scrimmage action-end values differed from recorded gain, all incomplete passes using the next eligible snap as fallback. These are state-linkage exceptions. The audit did not show that the ball landed at those incorrect spots.
- Of 4,250 eligible NFL main clips with valid timing, 482 ran longer than the provider's snap-to-whistle interval by more than 0.05 seconds. Of those, 149 ran more than a second longer. The largest difference was 3.112 seconds on a kickoff. The pacing policy deliberately retains natural movement when a generated clip takes longer. Keep that distinction when addressing separate try metadata and clock issues.
- The 420 rendered clip instances had no draw exceptions, missing atlas rectangles or missing held-ball anchors. The two matching art bundles exercised 12 of 14 eligible script templates. Spikes and the two eligible penalty clips were covered by server checks. Private art is installed for two of the audited 31 games, so the other games require a bundle to show a field.
- Animation and art both returned 401 without a session. The isolated signed-in Chicago–Seattle response was 1,463,037 bytes for 189 entries, with no HTTP content encoding on the measured requests. Cold and warm HTTP reads took 846 and 73 ms in that local run. Each relevant event refetches the full response. Treat those readings as a local cost sample and measure incremental delivery on a live slate.
- The focused Python suite passed 447 tests and the isolated member Chromium suite passed 11. The controlled replay probes reproduced failures that those suites do not cover.

## Suggested implementation order

1. Protect finalized NFL revisions, then reconcile Jacksonville–Baltimore through the normal authorized ingestion path.
2. Stabilize replay identity, reduced-motion following and the terminal canvas frame.
3. Recover spot-backed plays and tries with existing source facts. Add board-only stoppages and final.
4. Implement direct snaps, laterals, blocked punts and complex fumble variants from verified action sequences. Add private review clips and extend the rule checker to fail on regressions.
5. Close field-art availability and measure actual Sunday app latency with more than one game active.
