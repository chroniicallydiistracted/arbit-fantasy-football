# Task: Animate every play automatically as it arrives

Status: **proposed**, 2026-11-03, revised the same day after the owner's review (OWN-030). The owner asked "now we must figure out how to automate the creation of each play as they come in, so we can have animations on the fly." Nothing here is built yet. The CHI at SEA plays were choreographed by hand in `scripts/sprites/real_plays.py`; the owner accepted their look and uniforms, made the full-field view the standard, and asked for other players on the field, a jumbotron, and a field that is "truly animat[ed] ... live/interactive" rather than a GIF per play.

- **Requirement IDs:** LV-003 (play graphics, unknown fallback, keyboard and reduced motion), LV-004 (Gridiron art with a labeled fallback), LV-005 (late, revised and cancelled plays), LV-002 (own-roster filter on the same plays).
- **Specifications and decisions:** OWN-015 (Gridiron art private), OWN-019, OWN-021 (only named players, replaced by OWN-030), OWN-022–028 (sprites, ball, real uniforms), OWN-029 and OWN-030 (full-field view, other players, jumbotron, interactive field), ENG-023 (re-creation label), ENG-027 (painting), ENG-029 (depth order and kicks), [Live prerequisites](../../research/live-animation-prerequisites-2026-10-31.md), [real-time plan](2026-10-31-realtime-sync.md), [scoreboards](2026-11-01-live-scoreboards.md).
- **Baseline:** the worker already stores every play as append-only revisions (`nfl_play_revision`) and pushes changes to open pages over SSE. The median play reached us 36 seconds after it happened on 2026-11-01 ([receipt](../../research/live-slate-receipt-2026-11-01.md)). Frames, region maps, anchors and the ball are in `assets/sprites`. The Python review renderer is `scripts/sprites/preview.py`.

## How it works

```text
ESPN plays ──worker (exists)──▶ nfl_play_revision
                                     │
                        interpreter: revision → play facts          server, one implementation
                        choreographer: facts → play script          server, deterministic
                                     │
Gridiron week page ──new lane──▶ listing per game ──▶ measured colors, field file   private
                                     │
API (session-checked): scripts, art listing, field image ──SSE "play changed"──▶ browser
                                     │
Browser renderer (WebGL2): sprites + regions + colors + field + script ──▶ animation
```

1. **Interpreter.** Turns a stored play revision into facts: kind, offense and defense, start and end spot, line to gain, named players and their roles, the catch spot from ESPN's live yards after catch (ENG-032: on 506 of 508 receptions, within a yard of the official value on 19 in 20), depth and side of a pass (an incompletion aims within −4 to 13 air yards if short and 17 to 42 if deep), run direction, kick distance and result, return, penalty and turnover. Everything comes from ESPN's fields and text. A fact the play doesn't state stays unknown; a play missing a fact its template needs gets no animation and shows its text with the reason (LV-003 unknown fallback).
2. **Choreographer.** Turns facts into a play script: each named player's timeline of poses and positions, the ball (held, thrown, kicked, loose), the lines, the caption and the label. It generalises today's hand-written pieces (`throw`, `catch`, `tackled`, `tackler`, snaps, holds, kicks) into one template per play shape. It lands exactly on ESPN's spot and solves timing so a tackle happens there, the ball meets the receiver, and strides match ground speed. Generated choices, such as how far across the field a "deep left" pass goes or when the throw comes, are seeded by play ID and revision. The same play then looks the same everywhere, and a corrected play changes only when its facts change.
3. **Art listing.** A supervised worker lane reads Gridiron's week page on a declared cadence. It records each game's listed uniform per team and field, with status: listed, TBA, unlisted or two versions. It rechecks until everything is listed, then measures colors from the owner's local set with the code in `scripts/sprites/uniforms.py`, moved into a library. Fields usually post after the game, so during a live game the field is often not listed yet.
4. **Labeled fallback (LV-004, OWN-028).** Until a game's art is listed, its plays render on the plain drawn field in plain light and dark kits, labeled "Uniforms not listed yet" or "Field not listed yet". When the lane sees the listing, open pages switch to the real art without a reload. Nothing is inferred: no team colors from ESPN, no guessed field. 10 of the 32 home stadiums have only one 2026 field in the owner's set, but that is still not a listing.
5. **Renderer in the browser, not video files.** GIFs exist only so the owner can review plays before the app shows them; the app never plays one. The client draws each play from its script in real time. A script is a few kilobytes, while the review GIFs are 0.3–10 MB each and a week has about 2,000 football plays. Drawing in the browser lets the camera fit the screen, lets corrections and late-listed art re-render, and keeps Gridiron art behind session-checked routes instead of in files that could be shared. WebGL2 draws the field as a textured plane in perspective, which a 2D canvas can't, and needs no WebAssembly, so the current `script-src 'self'` policy holds. Sprites are painted once per frame, uniform and skin into a texture and drawn with nearest sampling.
6. **Framing per screen.** The full-field view is the standard (OWN-030). It puts players about 35 pixels tall on a 1,920-pixel frame, which holds on a computer and on a phone turned sideways. Held upright, a phone would make them about 7 points tall, so only there the camera follows the ball and shows about 45–50 yards; turning the phone gives the full field. The rule is to keep players at least about 24 points tall.
7. **A live field that persists between plays.** Each open game page holds one scene, not a list of clips. Between plays the ball rests on its spot, the lines show the next down, and the players walk back and set for the next snap. When a play arrives it animates from there, then the scene holds its result until the next one. Any earlier play can be replayed, paused, stepped or scrubbed, by pointer or keyboard; choosing a named player shows ESPN's facts for the play. Reduced motion shows each result in place with its text. A revised play re-renders with an "Updated" mark, and a cancelled play leaves the queue with a note (LV-005).
8. **Supporting players (OWN-030), on hold (OWN-032).** A family member found plays easier to follow with only the players in the play, so plays keep showing only the players ESPN names. The design is kept here in case the owner brings it back. Scrimmage plays would show 11 against 11, and kicks show both full units. The players ESPN names keep their exact roles, spots and name tags. Everyone else is placed by formation and moved by role, without name tags, under the same "Re-creation from play-by-play" label. Unnamed players never touch the ball or make a tackle, so they can't contradict the play. The interpreter's facts choose the formation: ESPN's text says "Shotgun" on 1,042 of the 1,714 stored scrimmage plays, and the run direction or pass side picks where the line moves. A fact that isn't stated uses one fixed default, seeded by play like every other choice. The motions: linemen pass-set or drive-block, defensive linemen rush and engage, receivers run routes, defensive backs cover and then break on the ball, and everyone chases the carrier and pulls up at the whistle. This needs new poses drawn the PixelLab way the owner approved (OWN-026): a three-point stance, pass set, drive block, two players engaged, a pass rush, a backpedal and a shuffle.
9. **Better formations for finished games.** No live feed gives per-play positions, and nothing we store does after the game either. nflverse's `nextgen_stats` holds weekly averages per player (time to throw, separation, cushion, rushing yards over expected and so on), checked in the three stored tables: no coordinates. Its `participation` data has no 2026 file and is published after the season. FTN's per-play charting is the best source: 2026 weeks 1–7 were stored on 2026-10-29, a few days after each game. It gives the starting hash, the quarterback under center, in shotgun or in pistol, the number of backs, defenders in the box, pass rushers and blitzers, and whether there was motion, play action, a screen or an RPO. When it arrives, a finished game's plays are rebuilt with those facts. That gives better formations, still not real positions. FTN data is CC-BY-SA, so its attribution shows wherever it is used. The NGS site does serve real tracking, but only for highlights: 9 of the 159 football plays in CHI at SEA, with player x and y and no ball height. NFL's terms prohibit collecting it into a database without consent, so it is the owner's decision (Q-011, [research](../../research/ngs-highlights-2026-11-03.md)). The owner chose to skip it for now (OWN-032). If it's approved later, a highlight would use its tracked paths and say "Tracked positions"; every other play stays a re-creation. Live movement, speed and catch-to-carry data, as the owner would like, has no live source.
10. **Jumbotron (OWN-030).** A stadium board fills the dark space above the far sideline in the full-field view. It's drawn in pixels on the players' grid, so it belongs to the same scene. It shows both teams' abbreviations in their measured uniform colors, the score, quarter, clock, down and distance, the ball spot, possession and timeouts left, as they were at the play on screen. A replayed play shows its own moment. After a scoring play or a turnover, it shows the result in large type ("TOUCHDOWN", "FIELD GOAL GOOD", "INTERCEPTED"), then goes back to the score. Each play revision already stores the scores, period and clock, and the game's `situation` stores down, distance, possession and timeouts. The letters are our own pixel glyphs, with no licensed font and no team logos. The board is decoration: the page's scoreboard stays the readable, accessible source of the same facts, so the board is hidden from screen readers.
11. **Kick results (ENG-029, ENG-030).** Passes, snaps and punts fly nose-first along their arc, with only a slight wobble; place kicks tumble end over end; the ball stands upright for the hold. A good kick crosses the end line between the uprights and above the crossbar, keeps its arc into the net three yards behind, and drops. For an offense going right, the kicker's left is the near side, so a miss wide left passes in front of the near upright. "Hit Left Upright" bounces off that upright, a short kick falls in front of the crossbar, and a block deflects at the line. The stored games have 59 good extra points and 3 missed, plus 11 missed field goals, 8 of them wide.

## Slices

Each slice ends with evidence. The owner review gate for new play shapes still applies: generated plays are judged as rendered GIFs before the app shows them. Every review render uses the full-field view.

1. **Interpreter and facts contract.** `libs/arbit-data/src/arbit_data/live/play_facts.py`. The normalizer starts keeping `yardsAfterCatch`, and the stored raw summaries fill it in for earlier plays. Tests take real stored plays as fixtures, one per shape and per text form. Evidence: a coverage table over every stored play, with facts checked against ESPN's own start and end spots.
2. **Choreographer for pass, run, sack and interception.** `libs/arbit-data/src/arbit_data/live/choreography.py`, script schema in `packages/contracts`. Tests check rules, not numbers copied from the code: the play ends on ESPN's spot, only named players appear, the ball arrives with the receiver, nobody slides, and the same input gives the same script. Evidence: the whole CHI at SEA game rendered from generated scripts by the Python renderer, reviewed by the owner beside the hand-made seven.
3. **Kicks, fumbles and penalties.** Kickoff, punt, field goal, extra point and two-point try with every result in item 11, then fumbles and penalty flags with enforcement. Same evidence as slice 2.
4. **Supporting players, on hold (OWN-032).** Skipped unless the owner brings item 8 back. The new poses, drawn and reviewed first as a sheet. Then formation templates and role motion for scrimmage plays and kick units (item 8). Tests check that every play shows 11 per side, unnamed players never hold the ball or make the tackle, nobody passes through anybody, and the named players' timelines are unchanged. Evidence: the whole CHI at SEA game rendered with full teams, reviewed by the owner.
5. **Jumbotron.** Board, glyphs and result cards rendered into the review GIFs from each play's stored state (item 10). Evidence: the owner reviews it on the CHI at SEA game.
6. **Gridiron lane and private delivery.** Worker lane, listing table (migration `0008`), measured colors, and session-checked routes for listing, colors and field image with `Cache-Control: private`, outside the service worker. Evidence: a full week's listings with status, rechecks observed until settled, and a blocked request without a session.
7. **Browser renderer and live field.** `apps/web/src/live/` with WebGL2: the persistent scene, play queue, jumbotron, replay and scrub controls, reduced motion and keyboard controls, and SSE-driven updates (item 7). Evidence: the same script rendered by the browser and the Python renderer on the plain field, compared frame by frame; the browser suite on an isolated port; frame rate measured on the owner's phone and computer.
8. **Live slate proof.** A real Sunday slate. Evidence: delay from ESPN's play time to the animation, corrections observed, no failed renders, and plays without a template counted and shown as text.

## Inputs, migration and decisions

- **Owner:** confirm the labeled fallback in item 4, or choose to wait for listings. Decide how skin tone is chosen (R-037); it matters more with 22 players on the field. Say whether supporting players (item 8) should come back. The pixel field (2026-11-03 handoff) is still optional; 2D is dropped (OWN-030).
- **Upgrades:** slices 6 and 7 change the running app, and slice 6 adds a migration. Both need the owner's approval at the time, outside game windows.
- **Rollback:** scripts are computed from stored revisions on request, so turning the renderer off loses nothing. The listing table is additive.
- **Not in scope:** any claim of tracking or real player positions, players ESPN doesn't name (OWN-032), and NGS tracking (Q-011).
