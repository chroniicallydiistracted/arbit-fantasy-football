# Live Game animation rebuild

November 7, 2026 · Approved · M6 · LV-003 and LV-005, with LV-001, LV-002 and LV-004 at their interfaces

## Intended experience

A member can read the real play and watch a plausible game-speed re-creation. It shows the credited participants, correct ball and possession changes, and supported spots and outcome. Players start in a plausible alignment, accelerate, change direction, react to one another, and transition between ready, moving, contact and recovery poses. Throw, catch, handoff and recovery share a visible ball location with the actor. A player can change roles within one continuous track. A player credited as both the intended target and the tackler on an interception return runs the route and pursues the returner as one actor. The same identity and evidence rules apply to all players.

The field keeps the existing pixel-art broadcast style, Jumbotron, private art, replay controls and reduced-motion presentation. The re-creation label stays visible. Cast size follows the existing rule to draw involved players. An unknown formation, route or supporting player assignment can constrain the inference and be omitted from the cast. The project uses a new version of the choreographer and renderer under the existing game page.

## Evidence and action sequence

The NFL detail feed is primary. ESPN remains the emergency fallback. Each adapter supplies its typed identities, description, state and timing evidence where available. Interpretation emits an ordered action sequence alongside official before/after state. Retain four evidence states at the field level. Observed means supplied by the provider, derived means computed from consistent provider facts, estimated means selected by the solver, and unknown means evidence is missing or contradictory. Evidence points to a provider revision, source field or description clause. Member-visible text continues to show the authoritative narrative. A missing critical anchor or conflict yields a labeled partial or text replay.

Use source identities internally with a verified link to canonical ESPN IDs where available. Keep display aliases, club and roles distinct. Prefer a provider's structured roles over matching names from the description. Preserve the intended receiver role from NFL stats and keep phase-specific roles attached to the same actor. An unresolved provider ID can identify a single actor in a play without asserting a league roster match. Resolve text-only participants only where the side, role and identity are sufficiently constrained. Retain raw timing, numbered text, structured yardage and relevant role evidence before choosing which constraints to apply.

Define events for snap/kick, exchange, release, arrival, possession, first contact, loose ball, recovery, out of bounds, score and dead ball. Each possession transition identifies the team and actor when known. Review and penalty effects stay attached to the effective revision. Distinguish physical endpoint, official/enforced spot, next snap and display state. For a corrected kickoff return with a fumble, preserve kick, catch, return, fumble and recovery in that order. Preserve the final ruling and its board effect independently of which of those actions can be animated.

## Motion and timeline

The solver first locks supported landmarks and the overall play duration when trustworthy. It selects phase windows jointly, considering the play description, personnel context, field boundaries and reachable paths. Then it solves continuous paths with feasible speed, acceleration, deceleration, lateral motion and turn radius. Use a small set of football movement patterns with context-dependent variation. Alignment comes from the reported formation cues, inferred spacing and role constraints. Avoid assigning a coverage matchup or blocker purely because a player receives a tackle credit. Routes and pursuit should be viable in the available time, and contact is shared by the actors involved. Use separate body heading and travel direction for facing the play while shuffling, backpedaling or turning.

The approved design replaces the former fixed handoff deadline with formation-aware exchange windows and replaces the center's mandatory hold in the snap pose with role-appropriate follow-through. A follow-through can be a brief set or a plausible release when the play context supports it. The center's exact assignment stays inferred. Deterministic choices use a stable play identity and stable motion evidence. A clock, text or board revision that does not change the motion inputs should preserve the path.

The timeline separates provider timing, ball-dead or scoring event, estimated motion end and post-play display. When motion overflows the available time, shorten estimated detours and phase windows within feasible limits. Keep the existing natural-speed last resort for a feasible late finish. Use partial/text when the available evidence cannot support a coherent action. Record fit residuals and downgrade reasons for validation. The board consumes independent official state and game-clock rules. Kickoff time before touch, administrative pauses, untimed tries and missing timing each have explicit handling.

## Rendering and playback

Introduce a versioned PlayScript and an opt-in script version on the existing animation endpoint. The current route's default remains compatible while the replacement is tested. The server produces the event and motion plan and the client samples it at a time. The browser resolves pose and directional sprite, foot placement and ball attachment from a shared scene transform. Attach the ball to the same hand or catch point at both sides of a state boundary. Provide distinct path shapes for snap, handoff, pass, kick, bounce and rest. Use pose metadata and directional frames where needed, with an authored fallback for gaps. Preserve the approved art character. No external art service or paid assets are prerequisites.

Use one projection for the field, actors, shadows, ball, posts and flags, with framing and depth ordering checked at the closest and farthest action. Review captures use the member renderer and a fixed sampling clock. Keep the current game-page controls and stable play/part selection. Apply motion-changing corrections at an explicit playback boundary. A cancellation or changed official outcome updates immediately and can replace the in-progress sequence with a corrected still or text. A stable revision stays visually stable. Key playback caches by content/version and bound their growth.

## Offline research and release gates

Use the owner's NGS archive offline to study formations, position and role spacing, route and pursuit geometry, timing and speed profiles. Match by game and play ID and verify source agreement before comparing. Split by game, hold out complete games and keep already studied examples as regressions. Use time windows defined by tracking events and separate arrival from possession. Use confidence and sample counts by context. The archive is selected highlights and has limited play-type coverage, so run ordinary PBP across the archived game corpus as a separate test. Tracking supplies x/y and event labels, with body heading, pose and height still requiring inference or independent film. Keep captures and derived measurements in ignored private data under OWN-053. Runtime uses current NFL/ESPN inputs and authored football motion rules. Offline measurements guide and validate these rules. Fitted tracking artifacts, derived tables and capture-specific paths stay in the private research environment.

Semantic tests cover ordered action, continuous identity, possession, score, review, cancellation and uncertainty with synthetic fixtures. Motion tests cover feasible turns, continuity, snap alignment, shared contact, possession boundaries and missing data. Compare phase errors and path shape against the current renderer on the same private held-out game set, stratified by play type. Establish tolerance bands from the development set, fix them before running the holdout, and report coverage and failure causes by category. Sample the real browser renderer on supported play families, both directions, mobile/desktop widths, corrected or missing plays and reduced motion. Check frame costs and update latency under concurrent games separately from offline accuracy. Use the isolated preview for mixed-version development. Before activation, the replacement must cover every play family supported by the current experience, with labeled partial or text fallback for individual missing or contradictory cases. Compare fallback counts by family to catch broad regressions. Activation requires owner review.

## Implementation boundaries

Sequence delivery through the evidence/action contract, continuous motion, the browser renderer and private regression review. Each slice has an independently verifiable output. Preserve the existing provider collection, canonical scoring and authorization boundaries. Exact solver tuning, renderer internals, compatible version negotiation details and sprite-frame choices belong to the implementation plan. The owner approved this design on November 7, 2026. Its handoff and center behavior supersede the relevant earlier motion constraints. The live path must work as soon as a play's current NFL revision arrives and should improve when later fields become available.
